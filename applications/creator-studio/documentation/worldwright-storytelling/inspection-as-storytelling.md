# Learning through inspection

**Date:** 5 November 2026  
**Status:** author direction and shared experience design; not implemented authoring capability

## Originating direction

Aaron clarified the purpose of linking Worldwright with Encounter:

> the whole point of the link is that it lets you inspect everything as you go
> little clues and hints
> a familiar syntax
> using things in a particular way
> kind of like it's training you as you are self-inspecting itself

The working interpretation is that exploration teaches a reusable grammar of inspection. The reader, author or participating agent learns how to look through the experience into the things, relationships and decisions composing it. That grammar can also disclose the composition and inspection apparatus themselves. This interpretation remains correctable by Aaron.

## The connection between products

Worldwright composes the clues, scenes, perspectives and encounters. Encounter supplies the general meanings and contracts that make an encountered thing inspectable, explainable and, where authorized, changeable. The reader experience becomes a reference case for learning that common grammar through use.

The link is therefore more than navigation between two websites or a shared backend. A passage can itself be inspected: why does it connect these scenes, who authored that reason, what kind of connection is it, and what will following it reveal? A title, diagram, source note or control can similarly open onto its actual owner and construction when the corresponding capability is available.

## Familiar syntax

The stable part should be the meaning of an operation. Its visual treatment can vary with the composition. Exact glyphs, gestures and labels remain design choices; the prototype's arrows and field-note buttons are not a frozen syntax.

| Reader question | Common operation | What should become inspectable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where does this lead? | Follow | The destination and the authored reason for the passage. |
| What am I looking at? | Inspect | The selected thing, its identity, perspective and current representation. |
| Why are these connected? | Explain relation | The relation's kind, reason and source; analogy remains distinguishable from derivation. |
| What supports this? | Open source | The relevant source or account and the scope of what it establishes. |
| What does another view reveal? | Change perspective | Another declared projection of the same identified subject, including meaningful differences or losses. |
| How did this become what it is? | Inspect history | Available predecessors, corrections and attributed changes at their actual retention scope. |
| What could I change? | Discover actions | Available operations for the exact actor, context and basis, including limitations or refusals. |
| How does this experience work? | Inspect composition | The scene, passage, representation or interaction definitions behind the current encounter. |

Repeated use makes this grammar familiar. A reader can recognize an opportunity to inspect before knowing what that inspection will uncover. The scene supplies a reason to try; the result teaches the operation through its actual effect.

## Clues and hints

An early scene can make one distinction easy to discover. A later scene can reuse the same operation in a less obvious setting. A return can invite the reader to notice something that was present earlier but had not yet become meaningful.

For example, opening the detector's field note teaches that an apparent observation has an underlying record and scope. Inspecting a passage into a personal account then reveals a different relationship kind. Later, inspecting a retained correction shows how an earlier interpretation changed. The familiar operation carries across these encounters while the meanings remain distinct.

Clues should reward attention without making essential orientation or source access a puzzle. The reader may inspect directly, follow a hint, remain on the surface or leave. Humour and the unexpected reflection in Aaron's horse-and-water image can invite recognition without a compulsory explanation of the lesson.

## A scene that exposes its own construction

The observation scene offers a concrete proposed sequence:

1. Read the title and try the detector illustration.
2. Inspect the illustration to distinguish an explanatory drawing from measured data.
3. Inspect a passage to discover why it leads to another scene and whether that connection is mathematical, editorial or personal.
4. Inspect the title to discover the actual scene subject, title property and its governing definition.
5. Discover an available title edit under the current actor/context/basis.
6. Form a proposal through the existing shared scalar-edit contracts.
7. Inspect the proposal's basis, intended change, reason and validation outcome.

Steps 4 through 7 depend on the authoring contracts and source-to-fact mapping identified in the [first scene-title edit](encounter-scene-title-edit.md). They are not capabilities currently supplied by the JavaScript reader. This sequence adds no special title editor, story mutation endpoint or alternative accepted-state store.

Inspecting a control's declaration follows the same principle: its representation, action binding and allowed operation should have recoverable owners. The depth of disclosure depends on the actual definitions and qualified capabilities; unexplained gaps remain visible rather than being filled by an invented source account.

## Learning and self inspection

There are three related movements:

- **Learning the grammar:** practice makes the reader or agent more capable of inspecting unfamiliar material.
- **Reflecting on an encounter:** the reader can reconsider their own interpretation or chosen route without the system claiming to diagnose their private state.
- **Inspecting the experience:** the system exposes the structure, sources and definitions composing the encounter, including how its inspection actions are represented.

These movements can reinforce one another. Following a connection teaches inspection; inspecting the connection makes the storytelling legible; that knowledge changes how the reader approaches the next connection. Reusing the same grammar for the apparatus makes the experience progressively understandable from within.

This is a learning-through-use design, not a claim of measured learning outcomes, automatic model training or completed self-hosting. An agent's suggestions and a reader's interpretations remain attributable contributions. Accepted system changes still follow their declared authority and retention path.

## Relationship to the existing contracts

Discovering editable actions, checking authority and returning a proposal remain the existing Slice 01 responsibilities. General inspection of composition definitions, history, source mappings and interaction machinery reaches further into Encounter's presentation, reflection, retention and self-extension design; those capabilities cannot be inferred from a passing scalar case.

The [Encounter reference declaration](../../../perth-revival-2027/docs/authoring-reconstitution/reference-apps/worldwright.md) remains the bridge to its source owners. No new surface or widened effect profile is introduced by this experience account.
