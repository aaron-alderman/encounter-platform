# Blinded Claimipedia test of “power serves”

## Primary result

The 24-record primary sample produced four service judgments, two control judgments and 18 unclear judgments. Neither reviewer used the mixed label in either panel. The primary sample fell short of the preregistered minimum of four records per class. The combined rule scored 62.5% balanced accuracy, below the 65% criterion, and control recall was exactly 50%, below the requirement that each class exceed 50%. The test did not establish reliable discrimination between service and control.

| Primary rule | Correct on 6 classifiable records | Incorrect | Abstained | Service correct | Control correct | Balanced accuracy |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Agency alone | 2 | 0 | 4 | 1/4 | 1/2 | 37.5% |
| Cost alone | 2 | 0 | 4 | 2/4 | 0/2 | 25.0% |
| Challenge alone | 0 | 0 | 6 | 0/4 | 0/2 | 0.0% |
| Combined | 4 | 0 | 2 | 3/4 | 1/2 | 62.5% |

Abstentions count as failures in per-class recall and balanced accuracy. Four of the 18 unclear records received a combined prediction. Three had agency signs and one had a cost sign. Those records have no binary reference label. There were five agency signs, three cost signs and no challenge signs across the full primary sample. All three cost signs were positive. The study therefore has no primary evidence about negative cost-bearing signs.

Cost answered two service cases that agency alone left open. One record asks for acknowledged repentance that costs the person or institution exercising power. Another presents Jesus as absorbing cost noncoercively. The combined rule still abstained on the use of religious symbols for regime ends and on a proposal to require public decision makers to justify coercion. Both records describe how power is oriented and give little direct information on how much agency affected people have, who bears a specific cost, or who can challenge a decision.

## Separate challenge-enriched panel

The primary feature annotations contained no explicit challenge signs. Before reading the primary judgments, a secondary 12-record panel was frozen using a vocabulary screen for contest, refusal and dissent. It used the same coding rules and rubric and was independently judged by a fresh reviewer. Its labels were nine service, one control and two unclear.

| Secondary rule | Correct on 10 classifiable records | Incorrect | Abstained | Service correct | Control correct | Balanced accuracy |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Agency alone | 3 | 0 | 7 | 3/9 | 0/1 | 16.7% |
| Cost alone | 1 | 0 | 9 | 1/9 | 0/1 | 5.6% |
| Challenge alone | 5 | 0 | 5 | 4/9 | 1/1 | 72.2% |
| Combined | 6 | 0 | 4 | 5/9 | 1/1 | 77.8% |

Five of 12 records contained usable evidence about challenge. All five agreed with the reviewer, four service and one control. The control example described an institution treating dissent as disloyalty. A service example treated disagreement and dissent as information and allowed unity to develop without prior enforcement. Challenge resolved two classifiable cases left open by agency. None of the rules made a prediction on the two unclear records. The secondary balanced accuracy is sensitive to its single control case and is reported descriptively. Two of its project records portray closely related restraint scenarios and both stayed in the analysis.

## Methods and verification

The primary 24 records were balanced across AI and society, Christian ethics and theology, and public institutions and conflict. The six classifiable records came from the latter two domains. The secondary screen yielded eight theology and four public-institution records. Claim identities, texts, rules and corpus hashes were frozen before annotation. Exact normalized text and human-confirmed equivalence blocked overlap with earlier tests. The two panels were not pooled. The supplementary screen was motivated by observed missingness in the first feature file, not the blinded labels.

One investigator manually coded both panels and recorded a verbatim source span for each nonzero sign. Two separate fresh Codex reviewers, one for each panel, judged the shuffled texts without the feature rubric, predictions, record identifiers, or source metadata. A replay reproduced both results. Separate checks verified the exact quote anchors, packet separation, coverage of 36 distinct records and score tallies.

The disposition is to retain the three questions as a way to read the claims. Cost and challenge recovered useful evidence when agency alone was silent. The strongest counterexamples are portrayals whose purposes are explicit but whose consequences or mechanisms are underspecified. The same text informs both feature and reference judgments, most records did not expose all three axes, and there were too few controls in either panel. The study does not establish out-of-corpus discrimination or the effects of actual exercises of power. No application or canonical claim was changed.
