# Advisory causal cues on Claimipedia records

The advisory checker retains exact typed source witnesses. It checks record revision, digest, quotation, span coverage, relation type, ordered roles, direction, modality and condition. The lexical cue is retained in the decision receipt and has no veto. The hard condition uses the previous checker unchanged. Both conditions receive the same proposals and witness file.

The frozen sample included nine hash-selected claims and two purposive existing effect-strength records found during retrieval. The effect-strength records were Claimipedia's school-network intervention evidence finding and the RIO-041 research-intake interpretation of resonance. Both were fresh relative to the earlier experiments and inspected before preregistration. The hash-selected texts were opened after the code, sources and sample were frozen.

## Ordinary proposals

| Arm | Supported links retained | Unsupported links | Meaning lost by the check | Total reference links omitted | Genuine strength links retained |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| No check | 15/16 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 2/2 |
| Hard whitelist | 8/16 | 0 | 7 | 8 | 0/2 |
| Advisory cues | 15/16 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2/2 |

One of 11 records had an unsupported link without checking and zero did with either check. Both checks removed a proposed causal edge that treated a characterization of cost displacement as scapegoating as a cause of scapegoating. The advisory check met the preregistered ordinary success condition. A link describing warranted exposure as enabling voluntary deference was omitted at the proposal stage. None of the three conditions added it.

In the school-network record, network position modifies the spread of an intervention's effects. The finding states that spread is moderated and gives no direction for the modifier. In the research-intake record, the resonant mechanical boundary amplifies the force-to-motion response. The hard whitelist missed both formulations. Advisory mode retained both typed strength assertions and all three additional supported links proposed on those two records. The hard checker retained one of those three other links. The test checked fidelity to the records' wording, including the attributed intake interpretation.

## Challenge and witness-error control

| Inputs and arm | Supported link instances retained | Unsupported link instances | Meaning lost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Eight challenges, no check | 8/8 | 8 | 0 |
| Eight challenges, hard | 6/8 | 0 | 2 |
| Eight challenges, advisory | 8/8 | 0 | 0 |
| Five type confusions with wrong witnesses, no check | 8/8 | 5 | 0 |
| Five type confusions with wrong witnesses, hard | 6/8 | 1 | 2 |
| Five type confusions with wrong witnesses, advisory | 8/8 | 5 | 0 |

The eight ordinary-witness challenges comprise three chain-to-strength confusions, two strength-to-edge confusions, and three invented edges on records with no reference link. The wrong-witness control repeats the five type confusions with deliberately incorrect yet exactly anchored witness signatures. The hard whitelist happened to veto four of these five errors. It let one through in RIO-041, where a word in the source window also matched the edge cue. Advisory mode let all five through. A typed witness can accurately quote a record and still misstate its meaning.

## Verification and checkpoint reflection

Replay matched the stored results and a separate link tally reproduced the ordinary metrics. The runner confirmed that the only decision difference was cue enforcement and verified the frozen inputs. Targeted checks rejected a source digest mismatch, an anchor without role-span coverage and a mismatched direction. The prior checker and prior artifacts were preserved.

One investigator annotated the references, ordinary proposals and source witnesses. The experiment measures behavior given those annotations and deliberate witness errors, with no estimate of independent annotator accuracy or the rate of unsupported links in production. Only two real effect-strength cases were assessed, and they were purposively selected. This result supports advisory cues as a way to avoid a measured lexical recall penalty when typed witnesses are sound. Its strongest counterexample is the wrong-witness challenge, where advisory cues remove a limited secondary veto. Retain as a research prototype for reviewed witnesses. No application integration or scientific claim admission follows.
