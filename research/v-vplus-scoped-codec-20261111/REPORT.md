# Scoped V/V+ reconstruction on fresh Claimipedia claims

The scoped codec adds reusable predicates with ordered argument slots, shared participant roles, possessive terms, explicit clause status, nested modality and negation, conjunction and disjunction, contextual and quantified scope, and directed relations between coordinates. A coupling can compose clause references, with no source text or new predicates in the link. The decoder receives only the structured encoding.

The codec, protocol and eight selected Claimipedia IDs were hashed before the selected texts were displayed. All eight were outside the earlier samples and known equivalence components. Four were from aiInquiry and four from claimipedia. I froze 29 essential probes before manual encoding. Both arms saw the same roles, coordinate material and asserted versus fragment statuses. Only the coupled arm saw the rules.

Coupling gained one probe each on three records, with no probe losses. It introduced unsupported assertions on three additional records. Both preregistered decision rules failed. Gains occurred on at least two claims, but exact reconstruction did not improve. The full arm also fell short of exact reconstruction on a majority.

## Verification and checkpoint reflection

The scorer replayed the output and checked the frozen hashes and deterministic selection. A separate check confirmed identical coordinate payloads between arms, reproduced the probe tallies, checked that changing role assignments, logical connectives or modal scope changes the controlled output, and verified rejection of invalid links. One draft encoding used an unavailable noun for contribution and failed validation before any decode. It was expressed as action of a contributor using existing terms. The codec, probes, selection and scoring rules were unchanged.

I wrote the probes, encoded the records and judged the visible arms. The score captures expressibility under this closed grammar and this investigator's coding choices. The difference in probe recall measures the incremental content of links in those encodings. An alternative encoding could place some of the same meaning within a coordinate. There is no same-record comparison with the first codec, so these results cannot estimate improvement over that implementation. The current holdout shows that ordered roles and scoped syntax do not compensate for missing domain referents, comparisons, precise qualifiers or an inaccurate role mapping. Retain the codec as a research checkpoint with demonstrated scope and role sensitivity, without a claim of general reconstruction, canonical admission, or application integration.
