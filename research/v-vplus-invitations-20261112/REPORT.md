# Blinded Claimipedia test of invitations and the ability to decline

## Outcome

The preregistered test ran on all 24 fresh records. The blinded reviewer found one clear invitation, 18 records with no invitation, and five unclear. The investigator coded seven invitations, 16 no and one unclear. Exact status agreement was 17 of 24. Among the seven records treated as invitations by either reader, the complete order sets agreed on two. One of those agreements was on a space-shaping mechanism. The other was an empty order set on a record the investigator regarded as an offer and the reviewer judged unclear.

No order reached the preregistered minimum of three reviewer positives. No reviewer-positive invitation carried multiple orders. The single shared positive was Claimipedia's Speculative Frontier, which provides a structured setting for people to develop, connect and stress-test hypotheses while keeping evidential obligations visible. Both readers classified it as space shaping.

All four investigator-coded influence cases were unconfirmed by the reviewer. The offered presence or self-salvation in several theological interpretations left the recipient or concrete uptake unclear. In the Gethsemane claim, the reviewer read permission to grieve as a principle about responding to suffering, with no participation or access arrangement. The disagreements identify an operational boundary between describing an attractive possibility and documenting an invitation to take it up.

## Refusal and the symbol controls

Both readers marked the shared space-shaping invitation's refusal terms **unknown**. The record describes what the platform enables, with no information about what happens when a person declines. The reviewer found **zero protected refusals and zero penalized refusals** among invitations. The six refusal-plus-consequence cue records supplied no reviewer-positive invitations. They included instances of an actor refusing to retaliate or shift cost, which give little evidence about what an inviter does when someone else says no. The preregistered refusal criterion was untestable on the resulting sample.

Both readers assigned no invitation and no order to all three symbol-control records. They concerned symbolic immortality, feeling as a signal of significance, and an experimental signature. Those controls show agreement about the absence of an offered action or operative audience in these records. They do not test a positive case in which a symbol functions as a contextual threat reminder.

## Methods, verification and checkpoint

The corpus, strata, 24 record IDs, rubric and runner were frozen before opening the sample. Exact normalized-text and reviewed-equivalence exclusions removed earlier records. The sample was cue-enriched, with several cues matching unrelated uses such as an offering as sacrifice, a scientific analogue, or experimental signature. Every record stayed in the analysis. The investigator froze source-anchored annotations before producing the packet. A separate fresh Codex reviewer saw only 24 shuffled text entries with opaque IDs and the rubric, without the prior conversation, strata or investigator annotations. Review included the basis of a claim, distinguishing portrayed or reported arrangements from proposals and conditionals. Neither label certifies that a consequence occurred.

A replay reproduced the stored scores and checked all source anchors, packet coverage and sample hashes. A separate tally verified invitation agreement and the symbol-control counts.

Retain the three mechanism definitions and the refusal distinction as research criteria. The test found strong disagreement on the prerequisite of a concrete invitation and too few clear examples to assess the full three-order distinction. Its clearest contribution is separating an opportunity described in text from evidence that the recipient can refuse it safely. Actual penalty-free refusal would require records that follow the same recipient, the option to decline, and the consequences of doing so. No application or canonical claim was changed.
