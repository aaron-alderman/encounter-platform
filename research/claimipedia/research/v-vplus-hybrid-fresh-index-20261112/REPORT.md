# Current hybrid on a fresh Claimipedia index

The current hybrid beat ordinary search by **five relevant results** at the same three-result limit. It met the preregistered criterion of a positive net count and more wins than losses. Query coverage was unchanged.

| Across 16 fresh queries | Ordinary search | Current hybrid |
| --- | ---: | ---: |

| Adjacent (grade 1) | 25/48 | 22/48 |

The hybrid won six queries, lost one and tied nine. It changed the third result on 11 queries, adding seven relevant pairs and displacing two relevant ordinary pairs. On three queries it fell below the threshold and used ordinary search's third result. On two other queries the highest eligible structural match was already third.

For the question of whether shrinking intervals between AI releases show takeoff, the hybrid surfaced a research rubric that treats partial signatures as ambiguous while rival explanations survive. The reviewer translated it into a concrete check: seek independent signatures and test alternatives before treating a fitted chronology as evidence of a mechanism. The displaced result about the continued need for courts and due process was judged irrelevant.

The loss was on the query about keeping speculative claims distinct from established support. Ordinary search found an institutional claim about fair notice, independent evidence and review, which gave the reviewer a specific route for revising a claim's status. The hybrid's substitute about repeated outcomes gave a more general connection and did not identify what evidence could change the status.

## Methods and checkpoint

The sample contained 16 new query records and 24 new candidate records across four editorial domains, including science and research strategy. The earlier vocabulary screen was omitted uniformly because too few unused AI and institutional-design records met it. Exact normalized-text and confirmed-equivalence components were excluded where they touched the earlier samples. Each arm searched the same 18 out-of-domain candidates per query. The investigator coded candidate frames before opening the queries, then coded and froze the query frames before ranking. Two candidates had no frame. All queries and candidates were retained.

The scorer reproduced the prior BM25 and current-hybrid rankings before opening the new test. The first two ordinary results stayed fixed in both arms. A fresh Codex reviewer rated all 59 pooled pairs under the previous rubric, with text and opaque IDs but no methods, ranks, structural scores or annotations. Replay and a separate tally verified the fixed result budget, coverage, swap accounting and score totals.

This is a fresh-query and fresh-index result with a modest gain concentrated in AI queries. One manual encoder, one AI reviewer, a 24-record candidate panel and related editorial domains limit the estimate. The broader eligibility and fourth domain make this a test under a changed query and candidate mix. It does not isolate which change explains the difference from the earlier runs. Retain the result as evidence for the fixed third-slot policy on this panel, with no deployment or scientific admission change.
