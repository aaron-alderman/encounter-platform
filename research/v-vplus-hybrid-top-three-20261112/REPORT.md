# Hybrid V/V+ and ordinary search at a three-result limit

## Result

The hybrid met the preregistered directional criterion by one relevant result. It added nine relevant third results and displaced eight relevant ordinary third results. The number of queries with a relevant connection stayed at 15 of 16.

| Outcome | Ordinary search | Hybrid |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Relevant (grade 2) | 29/48 | 30/48 |
| Adjacent (grade 1) | 14/48 | 15/48 |
| Irrelevant (grade 0) | 5/48 | 3/48 |
| Summed grades | 72/96 | 75/96 |
| Queries with a relevant result | 15/16 | 15/16 |

Hybrid won four queries, lost three and tied nine on the number of relevant results. It changed the third result on 15 queries, including five swaps of one relevant connection for another. One query fell below the preregistered score threshold and kept the ordinary third result. AI and society queries moved from 15 to 14 relevant results out of 24. Christian ethics and theology queries moved from 14 to 16 out of 24. These are descriptive splits.

For the query about decentralization in Judges, the hybrid retrieved a record about how distributed defaults and penalties shape independent judgment. The reviewer drew a concrete comparison: after removing a central ruler, check whether dispersed pressures still direct people's choices. The displaced search result dealt with misleading institutional success measures and received grade 1.

For the query about practical agency and nominal authorization, ordinary search retrieved a theological claim about restoring the conditions for agency instead of enforcing a preferred outcome. The reviewer used that distinction to ask whether a safeguard restores abilities such as veto and recovery. The hybrid substituted a claim about agency under constraints and the possibility of tragedy. The reviewer found the comparison too general to illuminate practical control.

## Methods and checkpoint

The protocol, candidate index, scorer and 16 fresh query IDs were frozen before the query texts were displayed. Each arm searched the same 20 cross-domain candidates per query. Ordinary search returned its first three BM25 results. Hybrid preserved the first two and used one V/V+ slot when the structural match met the frozen threshold. The candidate index and annotations were reused unchanged. The investigator manually encoded the fresh queries and froze their frames before ranking.

A separate Codex reviewer with a fresh context graded all 63 pooled pairs with the prior rubric. The packet contained only the two texts and an opaque ID for each pair, without method labels, ranks, frames or the prior conversation. The reviewer supplied a reason for every judgment. Replay reproduced the ranking, packet and scores. A separate tally verified the nine relevant additions, eight relevant displacements and the fixed three-result budget.

The query about non-coercive love and tragedy closely resembles an earlier indexed claim in the same editorial domain. The frozen exact-text and reviewed-equivalence filter did not catch it. That indexed claim was ineligible for this query under the cross-domain rule. The query remained in the analysis and tied. Institutional design supplied candidates, with no fresh queries in that domain under the frozen eligibility rules.

The result supports a small net advantage for this policy on these queries. It shows real swaps in both directions and a narrow difference relative to the manual coding and review effort. A single AI reviewer and reused 30-record panel leave reliability on new indexes and across readers unresolved. Retain this as a fixed-budget research result. The comparison does not establish a general improvement or justify promoting the hybrid to the default. No application or canonical claim was changed.
