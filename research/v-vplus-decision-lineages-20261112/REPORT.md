# Slopes, ratchets, and paths back in Claimipedia decision lineages

The study compared two ordered checkpoint cuts in each of five real lineages. It found descriptive agreement distinguishing observed from projected continuation, active from pending reversal burdens, and retained from technically conditional return paths. The clearest transitions were in bounded storage routing and predecessor custody. Most records gave less information about who could authorize a return or what it would cost.

| Field | Exact agreement across all cuts | Agreement when both readers assigned a known status | Known statuses shared by both readers | Preregistered descriptive criterion |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Slope | 8/10 | 8/10 | Observed, projected | Met |
| Ratchet | 10/10 | 4/4 | Active, pending | Met |
| Path back | 6/10 | 4/4 | Retained, conditional | Met |
| Chooser | 8/10 | 2/2 | Gated | Too little variation |
| Cost | 10/10 | 4/4 | Specific work | Too little variation |

For ratchet and cost, six exact agreements were shared unknowns. For path back, four disagreements came from the investigator treating an incumbent state that was still in place as a retained path. The reviewers reserved that label for records specifying how to return to that state. Neither a completed preparation step nor preservation of a predecessor proves a tested reversal.

The two readers agreed on the full early-to-late path label in two of five lineages and both detected the changes in those two. They agreed on the full ratchet label sequence in all five. The categorical ratchet label stayed active in the custody case because quarantine already imposed an extra restoration step. The evidence and explanations capture the increase after deletion.

## What the two documented returns require

**Bounded primary routing.** Activation changed the default to bounded storage while legacy remained synchronized. A real write then left legacy two admitted events behind, with a verified common prefix. A return preserving all writes now requires replaying those events into legacy, checking parity, and separately authorizing a pointer change. The records identify the human approval gate. They do not document an authorization to perform this return. Catch-up was exercised in a fixture, and the production return was not carried out in the supplied record.

**Predecessor custody.** Before permanent removal, an exact copy could be moved back from quarantine and the prior pointer restored where needed. Human confirmation was required before the proposed deletion, so withholding it would preserve that shorter return path. After deletion, the old pointer still named the predecessor, but the derived copy was gone. Returning would require reconstruction from retained source and recorded transformations, followed by separately reviewed pointer authority. The records document neither a complete post-deletion reconstruction nor its recovery time. The deletion approval gave no reconstruction authority.

Across the ten cuts, the reviewers assigned a gated chooser in only these two late records and no authorized chooser for a return. The early approvals related to forward actions. Specific return work was identified in four cuts from these same two lineages. The records supply no time or monetary estimate of the work or the person who would bear it. Storage reclaimed is a benefit of removal, separate from reconstruction cost.

## Methods, deviation, and verification

The source manifest froze 15 distinct documents, one earlier and one later cut per lineage, and the same return target across each pair. The five lineages were purposively selected after an eligibility and order scout. They are part of a related operational programme. Exact filenames did not appear in the prior reset and resolver study artifacts searched. The study does not assume independent organizations or generalize to other decision lineages.

Two fresh Codex reviewers worked separately. Each saw five opaque-ID cases containing ordered records and the common rubric. Neither saw the other cut, investigator marks, the paired key, or prior discussion. All 87 reviewer quotations were verified as contiguous source text and each observed slope cited at least two records. The investigator's first early draft failed exact-quote validation. A subsequent command in the same tool call displayed later material before the early annotation hash was written. The early status decisions were retained when the quote was corrected, but the planned investigator prefix lock was not achieved. This did not expose either blinded reviewer to the other packet. Different reviewers assessed the two cuts, so agreement on changes is descriptive.

Replay reproduced the scores and a separate tally verified the ten cuts, source spans, exact prefix relationships and paired accounting. No operational changes or live recovery actions were made. The result supports using separate fields for direction, reversal burden and path maturity. In these cases, observed progress could coexist with retained alternatives, and an apparent path back could lack technical prerequisites or authority. The strongest evidence concerned concrete work added by divergence and deletion. The chooser and cost estimates remained much less complete.
